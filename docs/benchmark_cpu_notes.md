# CPU benchmark: reading notes

Companion to `docs/benchmark_cpu.md` (generated by `python -m bench.speed`; raw
timings in `docs/benchmark_cpu_raw.json`). These notes are hand-written and report
what the numbers do and do not support. Conditions: laptop CPU (8 logical CPUs,
6 torch threads), fp32, the SFT checkpoint, K=4 prompts, G=64 generated tokens,
R=10 interleaved repeats, power and background load not controlled. Deviations
from the reference protocol are listed in its CPU addendum.

## What the data support (95% CI of the speedup excludes 1)

- **A KV cache is much faster than recomputing.** End-to-end, the static cache is
  2.3x faster than no cache for n=1 short (CI 1.98-2.45), 7.4x for n=8 short
  (6.68-8.20) and 7.9x for n=1 long (6.37-8.27); the tuple cache is 2.2x, 7.1x and
  6.8x. The gain grows with context length and batch size, as expected.
- **Static vs tuple cache depends on the cell.** No measurable difference at n=1
  short (1.06x, CI 0.93-1.27) and n=8 short (1.04x, 0.98-1.12). Faster at n=32 short
  (1.25x, 1.22-1.31) and in every long-prompt cell (n=1 1.16x, n=8 1.33x, n=32
  1.44x; decode alone 1.19x, 1.55x, 1.89x). The `torch.cat` cost grows with the
  amount of cached history and the number of rows.
- **Time to first token is not improved by the static cache.** It is equal in most
  cells and 8-10% slower in a few (n=8 short 0.92x, n=8 long 0.91x). A likely cause
  is allocating and zero-filling the whole `P+G` buffer inside the timed TTFT window
  (for example 335 MB at n=32 long); that cause was not tested.
- **int8 weight-only is slower on this CPU, as expected without a fused kernel.**
  fp32 static is 1.17-1.65x faster end-to-end than int8 static in every cell (and
  also in TTFT in most). int8 buys disk and memory (0.25x file size), not speed.
- **Batching raises throughput a lot.** Static fp32, short prompts: about 46 tokens/s
  at n=1, 245 at n=8, 622 at n=32 (per-step latency 21.5, 32.6, 51.5 ms).

## What the data do not support

- **Fine differences at n=1.** The n=1 cached cells have relative IQRs of 17-21%,
  so static vs tuple at n=1 is below the noise floor. Do not read the point
  estimates (1.06x) as a win.
- **Ratios from the re-runs.** The protocol re-runs a config whose relative IQR
  exceeds 20%. Those re-runs came out *slower* than the original runs (int8 n=1
  short 3.16 s vs 2.30 s; tuple n=1 short 1.80 s vs 1.48 s) while having a tight
  spread inside the re-run (3-12%). They run at the end of the benchmark, so the
  likeliest explanation is machine drift (thermal throttling or power state)
  between the interleaved main phase and the re-run phase, not a property of the
  config. The re-runs are therefore not compared against the other configs;
  all comparisons use the interleaved main phase. The size of the drift (up to
  about 35%) is itself a warning about absolute numbers on this laptop.
- **The no-cache cells beyond the reduced grid** (n=32 short, n=8 and n=32 long)
  were not run, so no claim is made there.
- **GPU behaviour.** Nothing here transfers to the T4: the cat-vs-static gap,
  TTFT cost and int8 speed can all differ on a GPU.

## Setup facts worth remembering

- The correctness gate passed everywhere: tuple tokens identical to static; the
  no-cache loop's teacher-forced logits within 1.2e-5 of the cached ones (limit
  1e-4); the real engine produced the same tokens as the benchmark's static loop.
- Long prompts use the first 256 tokens of validation stories idx 1, 189, 190, 191
  (the first four stories with at least 256 tokens), not the same stories as the
  short regime.
- The numbers are from a single machine on a single evening; repeat the benchmark
  before quoting an absolute figure.
